Open

Do not start with the product. Start with them. "Before I show you anything, I want to describe your Tuesday morning back to you, and you tell me if I have it wrong."

The thesis to land

Automation moves in three steps. Single tasks first, then whole workflows, then no interface at all. Step one is live and in production today. Step three is honest discovery.

Honesty point, set it up front

Say early that you will tell them what is real, what is being built, and what is still an idea. That framing buys you credibility for the next twenty-nine minutes and it is the whole reason they will believe the roadmap slide later.

Logistics

Twelve slides. Two of them are interactive, so invite them to point at things. Interrupt whenever.

The situation

Nobody here is short of systems

You are short of hours. The work still gets done, it just costs a good person most of their day to do it.

Planner
Three screens, one question
Checks the asset, then the contract, then the roster, to answer whether one job can move to Thursday.
What it costs
  • The answer was already in the data, in three places
  • By the time it is found, the customer has rung again
Buyer
Chasing a date by email
Third follow-up to the same supplier for a delivery date the purchase order already asked for.
What it costs
  • Nothing about the chase needs judgement
  • The part still lands late, and a van goes out twice
Service desk
The ninth time this month
Same fault, same customer type, same fix. Typed out again from scratch because the last nine are not searchable.
What it costs
  • Your best diagnostician solved it once, in their head
  • That knowledge leaves the building when they retire
None of this is a skills problem. It is a volume problem, and you cannot hire your way out of a volume problem. That is the gap we are aiming at.

Bridge

"So the unit of all of this is the digital worker. Let me show you what one actually is, because it is not what most people picture."

The building block

A digital worker is not a chatbot

It is assembled from three parts, and you configure them rather than code them.

80+ available
Agentic skills
Reusable sub-agents. Each one does a single capability well and can be dropped into any worker that needs it.
For example
  • Split a purchase order
  • Resolve a job to the right knowledge article
  • Run a three-way invoice check
100+ pre-built · 75+ connectors
Tools
The actions. Each tool does one thing against a system of record, and carries its own permissions when it does it.
For example
  • Read an ERP record
  • Create a purchase order
  • Trigger an IFS workflow
Semantic · episodic · procedural
Context memory
Starts empty. Learns your suppliers, your exceptions, your documents. The knowledge compounds and it stays with you.
What it remembers
  • Your process documents
  • Every exception a human corrected
  • Supplier-specific behaviour and quirks
The distinction that saves you money: a tool is deterministic, cheap and easy to audit. A skill reasons, and reasoning costs tokens. A worker built well uses tools for the boring 90% and skills only where judgement is genuinely needed.

05 / 12

Drive the diagram, do not read it

Hover two domains, no more. Field service and procurement are the pair that make the point fastest.

The line that lands it

"A sensor reading on a critical asset, owned by a cost centre, covered by an SLA, means something completely different to the same reading on a spare sitting in a depot. A model reading documents cannot tell those apart. This can, because the relationship is in the data model, not in the prompt."

Why this slide matters commercially

Four claims collapse without this layer. Industrial, because it is an asset ontology and not embeddings. Enterprise-grade, because permissions and audit attach to the edges. Headless, because the state of the work lives here and not in a form somebody is halfway through. Agentic, because planning is walking the graph.

Honesty point

The graph needs their data mapped to it. That is the real setup work in a pilot, and it is worth saying so here rather than letting it surface in week three.

Under the bonnet

Not an LLM bolted onto a workflow

Three design decisions, all aimed at the same thing. The same input should give you the same answer twice.

Fine-tuned 8–13B model
Planning orchestrator
A domain-trained model decides the sequence of steps. Planning has to be correct, not merely plausible.
Why it is separate
  • Variability in planning is a defect, not creativity
  • Domain training beats general fluency for this job
  • Single-model agents blur the two and drift
Episodic · semantic · procedural
Three-layer memory
Persistent and queryable, held in your tenant. Not trapped inside one model's context window.
What that gives you
  • Knowledge compounds instead of resetting
  • It is yours, and it does not leave with an employee
  • When an expert overrides a decision, the why is kept
Recognition over reasoning
Determinism
Known patterns get recognised and instantiated, rather than reasoned out from first principles on every run.
Why you care
  • Repeatable outcomes your auditors can follow
  • Far cheaper per run than re-reasoning every time
  • New patterns still get reasoned, then learned
The big general models still do plenty. They read the voice note, the photo and the PDF, and they write the reply a customer actually wants to receive. They just do not do the planning, because that is where being confidently wrong costs you money.

This slide answers the question they have not asked yet

"How is this different from us wiring ChatGPT into our ERP?" Somebody in the room is thinking it. Answer it before they say it out loud.

Keep it to one idea

Division of labour. One model that plans and must be right, one that generates and must be fluent. Everything on the slide is that idea in three forms.

Honesty point

Determinism is not absolute. It is a pattern library that covers the repeatable work and reasons through the rest. Say that. Overclaiming determinism is the fastest way to lose a technical audience.

07 / 12

Tell it as one job, not as nine products

"A pump goes down at six in the morning." Then walk left to right in one breath. Contact, triage, plan, parts, dispatch, close. The audience should feel the absence of the handovers.

The moment to slow down

The parts lane. Nobody asked for that requisition. The planning worker enriched the job, and the replenishment worker acted on it. That is the difference between nine tools and one workflow.

Impact, stated plainly

The value is not any single worker. It is that the context created at the first contact is still attached at the invoice. Put a number on it from their world if you have one, otherwise ask them what a repeat visit costs and use their figure.

Honesty point

This is Horizon 2. The individual workers are live, the full orchestrated chain is in build through FY26 and FY27. Do not let the diagram imply otherwise.

Who this slide is for

Usually one person in the room, and they have been waiting for it since slide one. Find them early and present this section to them directly.

The headline is the whole point

Adopting Loops does not change where or how they run IFS. That removes the biggest unspoken objection, which is that this is a migration in disguise.

Honesty point

The connectivity work is real and it is theirs. IP whitelisting or a reverse proxy is configured by their network team, and a site-to-site VPN is a joint build. Say that here rather than discovering it in week two of a pilot.

12 / 12

Close on them, not on the product

Four points, no new material. Then stop talking and ask the question in the callout out loud. The silence after it is the most useful ten seconds of the session.

Why "the most annoying one" rather than "the most strategic one"

Strategic processes have committees attached. Annoying processes have one frustrated owner who will give you their afternoon. That owner is how a pilot actually finishes.

Follow-up you owe them

Their IFS release checked against the support matrix, and the real go-live timeline for whichever customer reference they cared about. Write both down before you leave the room.
